# Investment by EDGE Program 
* Across all programs, EDGE has supported approximately $2.04 billion in total capital investment across Memphis.
* Most of this investment comes from tax-incentivized programs—PILOTs, Bonds, and TIFs—while direct financial support programs, such as loans and grants, represent a much smaller share.
* This pattern reflects the design and purpose of EDGE’s tools. Tax-incentivized programs do not require direct public spending, making them lower-risk and more scalable for large capital projects.
* These programs also align with EDGE’s mission to stimulate long-term economic activity, support job creation, and encourage place-based development that benefits surrounding communities—not just individual firms.

# Total Jobs by EDGE Program
* PILOT programs contributed the largest share of job creation, supporting 26,191 projected jobs.
* Other Projects followed with 16,105 jobs, reflecting the impact of targeted grants and need-based support.
* TIF districts contributed 7,661 jobs, driven by redevelopment and area-based investment.
* Bonds supported 1,900 jobs, typically tied to large industrial or infrastructure projects.
* ICED loans contributed 866 jobs, consistent with their focus on small business growth.
* Overall, PILOT programs are the clear leader in job creation across all EDGE incentives.

# Geographic Distribution of Investment
* EDGE projects are highly concentrated in ZIP codes 38118, 38104, and 38116, each with roughly 60–65 total projects. ZIP code 38118 has the highest concentration overall.
* 38118 is located near the Memphis International Airport and is dominated by distribution centers, manufacturing facilities, warehouses, and logistics hubs, making it a natural cluster for capital-intensive projects.
* 38116 contains the highest concentration of Industrial Revenue Bond (IRB) projects, reflecting its role as a major industrial and logistics corridor in Southwest Memphis.
* These patterns show that EDGE investment is geographically aligned with Memphis’s logistics economy, concentrating incentives in neighborhoods where transportation, warehousing, and industrial activity drive long-term economic growth.
